References

Annotated bibliography

Listen to this page

References are listed in order of first appearance in the tutorial. Each entry gives the title, authors, date and source, followed by a short synopsis and my assessment of its reliability.

  1. [1] "Depth Map Prediction from a Single Image using a Multi-Scale Deep Network," D. Eigen, C. Puhrsch, and R. Fergus, Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2014. Link

    Synopsis: The first widely cited paper to train a deep network to predict a dense depth map from one RGB image. It uses a coarse global network and a fine local network, and introduces a scale invariant error that later work built on.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed at a top machine learning venue and one of the most cited papers in the field. Older, so its accuracy numbers are far behind current models.

  2. [2] "Wearable assistive devices for visually impaired: A state of the art survey," R. Tapu, B. Mocanu, and T. Zaharia, Pattern Recognition Letters, Elsevier, 2020. Link

    Synopsis: A survey of wearable navigation aids for blind and low vision people, covering camera, depth sensor and ultrasonic systems and the design requirements such devices must meet.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed journal survey by researchers who have built assistive systems themselves. It reviews the field rather than proposing a new model.

  3. [3] "Towards Robust Monocular Depth Estimation: Mixing Datasets for Zero-shot Cross-dataset Transfer," R. Ranftl, K. Lasinger, D. Hafner, K. Schindler, and V. Koltun, IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence (TPAMI), vol. 44, no. 3 (arXiv 2019), 2022. Link

    Synopsis: The MiDaS paper. It trains one network on many depth datasets plus 3D movies by using a scale and shift invariant loss, and evaluates on datasets never seen in training (zero shot transfer).

    Reliability: High. Top journal, Intel Labs authors, open code and weights used by thousands of projects.

  4. [4] "Metric3D: Towards Zero-shot Metric 3D Prediction from A Single Image," W. Yin, C. Zhang, H. Chen, Z. Cai, G. Yu, K. Wang, X. Chen, and C. Shen, IEEE/CVF International Conference on Computer Vision (ICCV), 2023. Link

    Synopsis: Argues that metric depth is ambiguous mainly because of different camera focal lengths, and fixes this by transforming all training images into a canonical camera space before learning.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed at ICCV with public code. Needs the camera intrinsics at test time for the best results.

  5. [5] "Indoor Segmentation and Support Inference from RGBD Images (NYU Depth V2 dataset)," N. Silberman, D. Hoiem, P. Kohli, and R. Fergus, European Conference on Computer Vision (ECCV), 2012. Link

    Synopsis: Introduces NYU Depth V2, indoor RGB images paired with Microsoft Kinect depth. It is still the standard indoor benchmark for metric monocular depth.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed and used in almost every depth paper. Kinect depth is noisy and has holes on glass and dark surfaces, which limits how precise its labels are.

  6. [6] "Are we ready for Autonomous Driving? The KITTI Vision Benchmark Suite," A. Geiger, P. Lenz, and R. Urtasun, IEEE Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2012. Link

    Synopsis: A driving benchmark recorded from a car with cameras and a laser scanner. Its depth split is the standard outdoor benchmark for metric monocular depth.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed, maintained by the authors for over a decade. Laser depth is sparse and the viewpoint is a car roof, not a pedestrian.

  7. [7] "Vision Transformers for Dense Prediction," R. Ranftl, A. Bochkovskiy, and V. Koltun, IEEE/CVF International Conference on Computer Vision (ICCV), 2021. Link

    Synopsis: Introduces DPT, which uses a vision transformer encoder and a convolutional fusion decoder for dense tasks such as depth. It became the backbone of later MiDaS versions and of Depth Anything.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed at a top vision conference with public code and models.

  8. [8] "Depth Anything: Unleashing the Power of Large-Scale Unlabeled Data," L. Yang, B. Kang, Z. Huang, X. Xu, J. Feng, and H. Zhao, IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2024. Link

    Synopsis: Builds a data engine that pseudo labels about 62 million unlabeled images with a teacher model, then trains a robust student. Also reports metric depth fine tuning results on NYU and KITTI.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed at CVPR, from HKU and TikTok researchers, with open weights and reproducible benchmarks.

  9. [9] "Depth Anything V2," L. Yang, B. Kang, Z. Huang, Z. Zhao, X. Xu, J. Feng, and H. Zhao, Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2024. Link

    Synopsis: Replaces noisy real labeled images with precise synthetic images, trains a very large teacher, and transfers its knowledge to smaller students through pseudo labeled real images. Releases four model sizes and metric depth versions for indoor and outdoor scenes.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed at NeurIPS with open code and models. Some claims about fine detail are judged visually and on a new benchmark created by the same authors.

  10. [10] "ZoeDepth: Zero-shot Transfer by Combining Relative and Metric Depth," S. F. Bhat, R. Birkl, D. Wofk, P. Wonka, and M. Mueller, arXiv preprint (Intel Labs and KAUST), 2023. Link

    Synopsis: Pretrains on relative depth, then adds lightweight metric heads that predict depth as a mixture of adaptive bins. A small router picks an indoor or outdoor head per image.

    Reliability: Medium to high. Widely used and well documented open models, but published as a preprint rather than at a peer reviewed venue.

  11. [11] "Depth Pro: Sharp Monocular Metric Depth in Less Than a Second," A. Bochkovskii, A. Delaunoy, H. Germain, M. Santos, Y. Zhou, S. R. Richter, and V. Koltun, arXiv preprint (Apple), 2024. Link

    Synopsis: Predicts metric depth with sharp object boundaries and also estimates the camera focal length, so it does not need camera metadata.

    Reliability: Medium to high. Industry research with open weights; first released as a preprint.

  12. [12] "What Uncertainties Do We Need in Bayesian Deep Learning for Computer Vision?," A. Kendall and Y. Gal, Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2017. Link

    Synopsis: Separates aleatoric uncertainty (noise in the data) from epistemic uncertainty (gaps in the model's knowledge) and shows how to model both for per pixel tasks including depth.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed, foundational and heavily cited.

  13. [13] "Dropout as a Bayesian Approximation: Representing Model Uncertainty in Deep Learning," Y. Gal and Z. Ghahramani, International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2016. Link

    Synopsis: Shows that keeping dropout active at test time and averaging several forward passes approximates Bayesian inference, giving a cheap uncertainty estimate (Monte Carlo dropout).

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed and foundational. Later work shows the estimates can be poorly calibrated.

  14. [14] "Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty Estimation using Deep Ensembles," B. Lakshminarayanan, A. Pritzel, and C. Blundell, Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2017. Link

    Synopsis: Trains several networks with different random initializations and uses their disagreement as uncertainty. Often the most reliable simple baseline.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed, from DeepMind, widely replicated.

  15. [15] "On the Uncertainty of Self-Supervised Monocular Depth Estimation," M. Poggi, F. Aleotti, F. Tosi, and S. Mattoccia, IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2020. Link

    Synopsis: Compares many ways of estimating uncertainty for monocular depth (flipping, dropout, ensembles, predictive heads, self teaching) and evaluates them with sparsification curves and the AUSE metric.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed at CVPR with open code. Focuses on self supervised models trained on driving data.

  16. [16] "The Fourth Monocular Depth Estimation Challenge," A. Obukhov et al., IEEE/CVF CVPR Workshops, 2025. Link

    Synopsis: Reports results of a public challenge that tests monocular depth models zero shot on difficult scenes, and summarizes current trends such as metric depth and foundation models.

    Reliability: Medium to high. Workshop paper written by the organizers and teams; useful snapshot of the field, less detailed than a full paper.

  17. [17] "Learning Depth Estimation for Transparent and Mirror Surfaces," A. Costanzino, P. Zama Ramirez, M. Poggi, F. Tosi, S. Mattoccia, and L. Di Stefano, IEEE/CVF International Conference on Computer Vision (ICCV), 2023. Link

    Synopsis: Generates training labels for glass and mirrors by painting those regions with plain texture, letting a depth model predict the surface as if it were solid, then fine tuning on the result.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed with code. Needs masks of transparent regions during label generation.

  18. [18] "Digging Into Self-Supervised Monocular Depth Estimation (Monodepth2)," C. Godard, O. Mac Aodha, M. Firman, and G. J. Brostow, IEEE/CVF International Conference on Computer Vision (ICCV), 2019. Link

    Synopsis: Learns depth from ordinary video without depth labels by predicting how one frame warps into the next. Introduces tricks to handle occlusions and static frames.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed, widely used baseline with public code.

  19. [19] "Consistent Video Depth Estimation," X. Luo, J.-B. Huang, R. Szeliski, K. Matzen, and J. Kopf, ACM Transactions on Graphics (SIGGRAPH), 2020. Link

    Synopsis: Makes per frame depth geometrically consistent across a video by fine tuning the network on that video using structure from motion constraints.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed at SIGGRAPH. Runs offline, so it is not suitable for live guidance as is.

  20. [20] "FastDepth: Fast Monocular Depth Estimation on Embedded Systems," D. Wofk, F. Ma, T.-J. Yang, S. Karaman, and V. Sze, IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), 2019. Link

    Synopsis: Designs a small encoder and decoder network and prunes it so that depth runs in real time on an embedded NVIDIA Jetson board.

    Reliability: High. Peer reviewed, MIT authors, with released code. Accuracy is well below modern foundation models.
