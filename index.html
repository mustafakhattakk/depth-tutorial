Reliable Monocular Depth Estimation for Assistive Navigation

Metric depth, uncertainty and failure detection from a single camera, and what it takes to make them safe enough to guide a blind or low vision person.

By Mustafa Khattak, graduate research tutorial in computer vision, California State University, East Bay, Fall 2026

Figure 1. A hallway drawn as a depth map, the kind of output this tutorial is about. Warm yellow means near, deep purple means far. Source: original illustration by the author.

Listen to this page

A phone camera can already tell you that there is a chair in the room. This tutorial is about the harder question a blind or low vision person actually needs answered: how far away is it, and can the system be trusted when it says so?

We will move from the basics of single camera depth estimation to the models that define the current state of the art, then focus on three things that matter for safety: getting distances in real meters, measuring how confident the model is, and spotting the situations where it is likely to be wrong.

What you will learn

  • Why one camera cannot directly measure distance, and how networks estimate it anyway.
  • The difference between relative and metric depth, and how to convert one into the other, with working code.
  • How MiDaS, DPT, Depth Anything V2, ZoeDepth and Depth Pro differ and when to choose each.
  • How to estimate uncertainty, smooth depth over video, and evaluate a model honestly.
  • How these pieces fit into an assistive navigation system, including where they still fall short.

How to use this tutorial

Work through the ten parts in order. Each takes two to four minutes to read, so the whole tutorial takes about 25 minutes. Every page starts with an audio narration you can play instead of reading. Numbers in brackets such as [1] link to the annotated bibliography. Part 9 has an interactive guidance simulator, and a short quiz at the end lets you check your understanding.
