Limitations, current challenges and future research

Limitations of the systems reviewed

SystemMain limitationsEffect on an assistive app
MiDaS [3]Relative only, older versions blurryNeeds calibration before it can say "meters"
Depth Anything V2 [9]Metric models tied to indoor or outdoor training domains, large variants too slow for phones, no built in uncertaintyMust choose a model per scene and add uncertainty separately
ZoeDepth [10]Only two domains in its router, lower accuracy than newer modelsMixed scenes such as a station entrance can be misrouted
Depth Pro [11]Heavy, built for high resolution imagesBetter for offline scene description than live warnings
Uncertainty methods [15]Multiply compute, often poorly calibratedAdds delay, thresholds must be tuned on real data
Video consistency [19]Offline optimizationNot usable live without simplification

Three limitations apply to all of them. First, they estimate rather than measure, so no amount of model quality removes the risk entirely. Second, glass and mirrors remain unsolved in everyday use. Third, the standard benchmarks were captured from a car roof or a tripod, not from a phone held by a walking person, so reported accuracy may not match what a user experiences.

Current challenges and future research

More accurate metric depth from ordinary cameras

Estimating the camera's own focal length, as Depth Pro and Metric3D do [11] [4], removes one major source of error. I expect future phone apps to combine this with the camera metadata that phones already provide.

Better uncertainty estimation

Foundation models are released without uncertainty outputs. Adding a calibrated uncertainty head during their training, instead of bolting it on afterwards, would make them far more useful for safety critical use.

Reliable detection of transparent and reflective objects

Work such as [17] shows that labels for glass can be generated. A dedicated glass detector running alongside depth estimation is a practical near term step.

Faster models for phones and edge devices

Distillation, the same teacher and student idea used in Depth Anything V2 [9], together with quantization and pruning as in FastDepth [20], is the main path toward strong models running in real time on mid range phones.

Temporal consistency and sensor fusion

Video depth models that are both stable and fast are an active research area. Fusing monocular depth with an inexpensive range sensor offers the best of both: scene detail from the camera and a measured check where it matters most.

Research groups to follow

Intel's Intelligent Systems Lab created MiDaS and DPT, researchers at the University of Hong Kong and TikTok created Depth Anything, Apple's machine learning research team created Depth Pro, the University of Bologna has done leading work on depth uncertainty and transparent surfaces, and the organizers of the Monocular Depth Estimation Challenge [16] track progress across the field each year.

Conclusion

A single camera can now estimate distance well enough to be genuinely useful for navigation, something that seemed out of reach a decade ago. But "useful" is not the same as "safe." The systems that will actually help blind and low vision travelers are the ones that report metric distances, attach honest confidence to them, stay stable over time, and are willing to say "I'm not sure." Building those qualities around today's strong depth models is, in my view, the most important open problem in this area.
